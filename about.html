About

From robotics labs in Accra to full stack systems in LA

The dual background that shapes how I build and how I teach.

I'm a Master's student in Computer Science at USC, building on a Bachelor's in Information Technology from Ghana Communication Technology University, where I graduated with First Class Honours. My path into software didn't start with a job title, it started in robotics labs in Accra, teaching programming to students across more than fifty high schools before I ever shipped a line of production code.

That dual background still shapes how I work. I care about systems that hold up under real use, and I care just as much about whether someone else could pick up what I built and understand it. Whether that's a secure authentication flow, a spatial database query, an adaptive cruise controller tested against formal safety requirements, or a TinyML model running on a Raspberry Pi in a classroom, the standard is the same: does it actually work, and can someone learn from it.

Outside of engineering, I lead a church media team, run a small photography practice, and I'm a member of the National Society of Black Engineers. I'm drawn to work that sits at the intersection of technical depth and clear communication, because in my experience, the two make each other better.
